# Total Chlorine Documentation In-Service

## Problem

Clinical staff needed clearer guidance on Total Chlorine documentation requirements to reduce late readings and incomplete supporting records.

## Goal

Develop a standardized in-service document that reinforced the four-hour timing expectation, explained what to document when a justified delay occurs, and provided evidence that staff received the training.

## My Role

I identified the recurring documentation gap, translated the operational requirement into plain-language staff guidance, organized the content, designed the acknowledgement section, and prepared a sanitized portfolio version.

## Tools Used

- Microsoft Word
- PDF
- Operational report review
- Document-layout and information-design principles

## Design Decisions

- Placed the most important timing expectation near the beginning
- Used direct language emphasizing that readings should be completed early rather than late
- Separated routine expectations from justified exceptions
- Required both the paper log and typed note when a delay has just cause
- Added printed-name, signature, role, and date fields to document staff education
- Removed employer branding, facility identifiers, employee information, and proprietary references from the public version

## Final Deliverable

A one-page in-service and staff acknowledgement document containing:

- Training purpose
- Learning objectives
- Timing expectations
- Exception-documentation guidance
- Staff acknowledgement table

## Lessons Learned

A compliance document is more useful when it explains both the normal expectation and the required response when an exception occurs. A signature section also turns training activity into documented evidence.
